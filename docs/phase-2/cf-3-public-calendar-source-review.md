## Purpose

This review records the strongest external calendar evidence currently available for the first
connected synthetic calendar workflow. It does not convert public documents into reviewer
testimony. It separates directly published facts, cautious interpretation, and facts that the
sources do not establish.

Finalsite does not publish a complete public registry of every customer calendar. The inventory
below is therefore a confirmed, non-exhaustive set discovered through official school pages and
`resources.finalsite.net` school asset paths.

The two records differ in primary-period count, closure treatment, tentative dates, and make-up-day
semantics. Their shared Monday–Friday grids do not prove that all institutions use those weekdays.

## Findings for the first contract

### Supported by the current date-level authority

- academic years crossing civil years;
- three- or four-period primary sequences with local labels;
- fixed date-level closures and non-student professional-development days;
- explicit gaps between primary periods; and
- exact published revision pinning for downstream attendance once the institution enters its own
  authoritative definition.

### Real patterns deliberately outside CF-3

- A/B, A–J, numbered, or other rotating-day cycles;
- quarters overlapping semesters or another simultaneous primary/secondary sequence;
- half-day instructional status and clock-time scheduling;
- grade- or cohort-specific first and last days;
- boarding, exeat, parent-conference, arts, athletics, and event calendars;
- tentative holidays and conditional make-up-day activation;
- subscriptions, Google Calendar, ICS, imports, and event-feed synchronization; and
- public-calendar editing or correction workflows.

## Unknowns retained

The public sources do not establish:

- who owns calendar publication internally;
- the institution's authoritative IANA time-zone setting;
- how a published mistake is approved and corrected;
- whether several calendars coexist under one exact institutional unit;
- which internal records pin which publication revision;
- whether Chimwemwe terminology is understood by representative operators; or
- whether the visible public calendar is complete enough for attendance authority.

These unknowns do not block the disabled local synthetic implementation under ADR 0039. They do
block claims of institution-side approval and must be settled with the first participating
institution before real data, import, pilot, deployment, or production reliance.

## Disposition

**Approve source-backed CF-3 engineering entry with retained adoption conditions.** The sources
are sufficient to challenge the first calendar contract and to build the local synthetic
same-origin workflow. No external representative review, Finalsite partnership, calendar-owner
approval, or production fitness is claimed.
